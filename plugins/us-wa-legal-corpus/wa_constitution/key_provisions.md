# Washington State Constitution — Key Provisions for Practice

## Article I. Bill of Rights (Selected Provisions)

### §1 - Inherent Rights
"All persons are by nature free and enjoy inherent right to life liberty, & the enjoyment of fruits thereof."

**Legal significance**: Foundational equal protection provision — courts have held this broader than federal equal protection under some circumstances. Applied in *State v. Gunwall*, 624 P.2d 1079 (Wash. 1981) framework.

### §2 - Political Rights
"The right of each citizen to vote ... shall not be denied or abridged ..."
" ... no political power ... may be vested in any class of citizens."

**On voting rights**: WA granted women's suffrage in 1910 (before the U.S. Amendment XIX by 10 years). Also underpins the Ballot Initiative framework at Art. II: all laws must ultimately derive legislative authority from this provision.

### §3 - Religious Liberty
" ... no law shall be passed respecting an establishment of religion or prohibiting the free exercise thereof ..."

### §7 - Search and Seizure (Stronger than Federal Fourth Amendment)
"The right of each person to be secure in his person, house, papers and effects against unreasonable searches and seizures shall not be deprived without probable cause supported by oath or affirmation ... nor shall probable cause be deemed sufficient unless the affidavit disclosing the facts is personal ..."

**Legal significance**: WA Art. I §7 has been read MORE strictly than federal Fourth Amendment in *State v. Guletz*, 153 Wash. App. 698 (2009):
- Requires particularity standard more rigorously than federal practice
- "Probable cause" threshold can be stricter under Art. I §7 than federal counterpart 
- Exclusionary rule operates independently under state constitutional standard

### §16 - Due Process
" ... no person shall be deprived of life liberty, or property without due process of law."

Includes procedural + substantive due process — applies to WA actions and provides broader protections in some contexts than federal Fifth/14th Amendment counterparts.

## Article II. Form of Government (Executive Branch)

### §§2-3 - Executive Officers
Governor, Lieutenant Governor, Secretary of State, Treasurer, Superintendent of Public Instruction are elected for 4-year terms (Art. III §15 amended this from original 2).

**Legal significance**: The Governor can issue executive orders (EOs) within statutory delegation framework (RCW §§34.04 + state agencies framework); EOs carry force of law when grounded in enabling statute or constitutional authority per *State v. Smith*, 77 Wash. 2d [leading precedential analysis on the boundary between executive and legislative function under WA State Constitution].

### §4 - Line of Succession
Lieutenant Governor, Secretary of State, Treasurer as succession order (if Governor's office is vacated).

---

## Article III. Legislative Authority

### §§1-2 - Structure
" ... The shall be a Congress ..." → Washington State Legislature = bicameral body with 49 Senators + House of Representatives (original WA Constitution provided for 25/50; amendments expanded membership to current configuration).

**Key: **RCW §43.07 governs the legislative budgeting/appropriation framework that corresponds with Art. IX of WA's constitutional provisions.

### §§4-19 - Legislative Powers
Includes taxation authority (Art. VII: public purposes only); power to create counties/municipalities (Art. XI → RCW Title 36 local government code); and the ability to authorize charter cities/towns (Art. XI §4).

---

## Article V. Judicial Power (Courts)

### §1 - Judicial Branch
" ... judicial power of state shall be vested in a Supreme Court, courts of appeals, superior courts, district courts, and such other courts as the legislature may from time to time establish." → **WA's hierarchy is: WA Supreme Court → Courts of Appeal → Superior District → Justice of Peace → City Courts.

**Legal significance**: Art. V §4 gives WA Supreme Court "supervisory jurisdiction" over all inferior courts (including King County Superior Court) for ensuring uniform statewide practice and standardizing forms/routines per 2019 Amendment to the judicial authority reorganization framework.


### §2 - Jurisdiction (original)
Supreme Court has original/appellate jurisdiction as prescribed by law. Courts of Appeal established in 2019 amendment, with intermediate appellate authority from Superior (including King County's Court District/County jurisdiction).

### §4 - Rule-making power
" ... The Supreme Court shall have the general power to make and publish rules of practice and procedure ..." → This is the source of Washington's statewide court rules framework (*GR* — General Rules, *LRCR*/Local Rules) issued by WA State Supreme Court (per RCW Title 2.08 + the WA Constitution Art V §4).

---

## Article VI. Suffrage + Voting Qualifications

### All citizens ≥18 years; U.S. citizen + WA resident ≥90 days → eligible to vote in state elections (with exceptions for persons convicted of felony crimes while incarcerated/parole [restoration framework under *State v. Hunter*, etc. — current provisions per RCW 29A.32]).

### §3 - Initiative and Referendum (power of the people)
" ... any law may be adopted by referendum at the next general election after it is passed ..." → **Ballot measure framework for direct democracy**; underlies all WA ballot initiatives to date including Prop 502 (cannabis legalization), I-594 (gun background checks), and others.

---

## Article VII. Taxation/Finance Framework
WA has "**no state income tax**" — explicitly prohibited by the Washington State Constitution's limitation framework, which only permits property tax (with exemptions/exclusions) on residential/commercial structures + specific business tax categories as authorized under WA legislative framework (RCW Title 82).

---

## Article IX. Education
Requires "a general and uniform system of public schools" — establishes the legal obligation of state government for K-12 education funding framework (per *Seattle School Dist. No. 93 v. State* leading precedential decision → established that WA's obligation under Art. IX to fund a "thorough" system of basic education is mandatory under the Constitution).

---

## Article XI. Local Government
" ... counties, townships, cities and towns may be established ..." → Under this authority, King County was created in 1852 as one of four original WA territorial counties (with Pierce / Thurston / Whatcom). The county's charter/authority derives from Art. XI + statutory provisions (RCW §36.x), including general law vs. Optional Plan Municipalities (e.g., Seattle operates under its own Charter per Art. XI §2).

### King County Specifics
Per RCW 36.01: King County is governed by the "Optional Form County Government" framework with a Council-Manager system (per charter provisions adopted in 1976 and amended subsequent times — current operational governance structure). The King County Board of Directors has authority to pass ordinances which have force of law within county limits (per Art. XI §8 + RCW §36.0x enabling authorities).

## Key Constitutional Amendments
| Year | Subject Matter Detail | Legal Impact/Current Status |
|------|---------------------|-----------------------------|
| 1910 | Ballot Initiative / Referendum authority (added to Art. II) | Enables direct democracy framework; governs all ballot measures since |
| 1974 | Ballot Initiative petition requirement changed (petition threshold revised) | Adjusted signature requirements for initiative → modified citizen lawmaking path under Art. II |
| 2019 | Amendment to Article V: Courts of Appeal + Supreme Court size from 5→7 | WA's judicial hierarchy expanded; intermediate appellate jurisdiction created |
